# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- an automation profile's policy is kept in the selected store, SQLite or PostgreSQL: its defaults (a risk percentage, an optional fallback stop percentage, a leverage, and whether assets without settings of their own trade on them), the assets with settings of their own, each with a switch, and its risk capital in each settlement asset; every change is a new revision of the whole policy, stored beside the earlier ones and never changed, so any revision reads back word for word after a restart
- a profile belongs to one venue, environment, account and product family, so the same symbol on two venues, or in a linear and an inverse profile, shares no setting, no capital and no switch; a store takes profiles of the environment it is bound to and no other
- an asset's setting is its own or the defaults', each read back with where it came from, and resetting an asset puts every setting back on the defaults; defaults enable nothing until an operator switches them on, and a change that would leave something enabled without a risk percentage or a leverage is refused whole
- of two changes made from one revision of a policy one is stored, and the other is told the profile moved on or is made again on top of it, on both backends; no change is lost under another and no revision is skipped or written twice
- an admitted entry carries a snapshot of the policy it was admitted under, with the policy's revision and the revision that set its capital, and keeps it for good: an edit applies to later entries only, so a setting changed while a position is open resizes nothing and removes no protection
- a disabled asset, or one without settings while the defaults are off, takes no new entry (`not_armed`, `unknown_ticker`), and one without capital in its settlement asset none either (`no_capital_in_settlement_asset`); exits, monitoring and protective closes take no policy, so they go on
- an entry's stop is the alert's own first, then the fallback percentage of the asset or of the defaults, and without either the entry is refused (`no_stop`); nothing else a signal carries reaches the risk, the leverage, the capital, the account or a switch
- `automation::PolicyService` is what a command or an executor keeps policies through: it adds an asset as the venue lists it, refuses a change that would enable what the venue's instrument list does not take, and `check` says what keeps a profile from executing: a contract the venue does not list or keeps outside the universe, a changed settlement asset, a leverage outside the venue's range or off its step, or missing capital; no command reaches it yet
- `PolicyStore` is the storage port for profiles and policy revisions, implemented by both backends and held to the shared conformance group `policies`, racing writers included; `docs/automation-policy.md` (policy format 1) is the contract, held to the code and to `tests/fixtures/policy_cases.json`, cases an independent model writes for all four futures product lines
- `data migrate` copies the new `automation_profiles` and `policy_revisions` tables, and `--verify` compares them
- a backtest job has a log a client reads while the job runs and afterwards: `GET /v1/backtests/{job_id}/logs` answers a page of it, the lines after the query's `after` cursor, at most `limit` of them (1 to 1,000), each with its number, the server's time, its level (`debug`, `info`, `warning` or `error`) and its text, then the job's progress as the bars it has run of the bars it runs on, the cursor to ask with next, and `complete` once the job has ended and no line follows
- the same path asked as a WebSocket upgrade follows the log: a `line` frame for each line after the cursor, first the ones the server holds and then each as the job writes it, a `progress` frame whenever the job has come further, and `end` once the job has ended and its last line was sent, after which the server closes; the stream of a queued job waits for the job, a stream with nothing to send pings its client every 30 seconds, a client that lost its connection asks again with the number of the last line it has, a server that stops closes the stream of a job that has not ended with close code 1001, and a stream the server cannot go on with ends with an `error` frame
- a job's log is bounded whatever the job writes: the server keeps the newest 10,000 lines and 1 MiB of text of each job, and the first 8 KiB of a line, and what it leaves out is counted, never passed over in silence, as a page's `dropped_lines`, a stream's `truncated` frame and a line's `dropped_bytes`; a job that writes without end waits on its own output, and a client that reads slowly, or not at all, costs the server no more memory than one that keeps up
- a job's log is kept in the selected store, SQLite or PostgreSQL, once the job has ended, however it ended, and written before the job's end is, so whoever reads that a job has ended finds its log, and every server on the store replays the same lines, a page at a time and as a stream; a job that never started has an empty log, and so has one whose server was killed while it ran
- a worker sends its server what its job says while it runs, each line with its level and how many of its bars the job has run, and a backtest says how far it has come about every hundredth of its bars; the server holds a worker's count of bars to the job's and fails a job whose worker sends a level it does not know. This release's workers still offer no runtime, so `serve run` fails each job as it starts and no job of its own writes a log yet
- `protocol::jobs` specifies the log, with `LogQuery`, `LogPage`, `LogLine`, `LogLevel`, `JobProgress`, `LogFrame` and `LOG_PAGE_LINES`; `server::JobLog` is the log a server keeps while a job runs and `server::LogLimits` what it keeps of one, which `JobLimits::log` sets
- `data migrate` copies the new `job_logs` table, and `--verify` compares it
- a backtest job that succeeds has a result a client downloads: `GET /v1/backtests/{job_id}/result` answers its metrics (initial cash, final equity, total return, maximum drawdown and trade count) and where its three result files are, and `GET /v1/backtests/{job_id}/result/metrics.json`, `.../trades.arrow` and `.../equity.arrow` send them; a job that has not succeeded answers `409 conflict`, saying whether it is queued, running, failed or cancelled
- `trades.arrow` and `equity.arrow` are Arrow IPC files (`application/vnd.apache.arrow.file`) that pyarrow, pandas, Polars and arrow-rs read as they are: one row per closed trade, and one equity row per bar the job ran on; times are `Int64` milliseconds, and every price, quantity and amount is its exact decimal text in a `Utf8` column, trailing zeros kept, never a float or an Arrow decimal
- `protocol::results` is the contract a client decodes against, with `Metrics`, `TradeRow`, `EquityRow`, `TRADES_COLUMNS`, `EQUITY_COLUMNS` and strict `encode_*`/`decode_*`; `docs/result-spec.md` (result format 1) fixes every byte of each file, so the same result is the same three files on every server and store and a file's hash names it, and `tests/fixtures/result_cases.json`, written by an independent Python implementation, holds the Rust code to them
- a job's result is kept in the selected store, SQLite or PostgreSQL, in the transaction that marks the job succeeded, so it outlives the server that ran the job and a job never lists a file the store does not hold; each file is stored once under its SHA-256, as one zstd frame, and the server checks it against that hash before it sends a byte
- a result file is sent with `Cache-Control: private, max-age=31536000, immutable` and the entity tag `"<hash>"`, and a request whose `If-None-Match` names that tag is answered `304 Not Modified`
- `BacktestResult::equity_curve` is the close-marked equity a backtest computes its drawdown on, one `EquityPoint` per bar; it leaves out the fee of a close-out at the end, which `final_equity` includes
- `data migrate` copies the new `job_results` table, and `--verify` compares it
- a worker hands its server a backtest's summary and rows, never files: the server checks each row and writes the result files itself. This release's workers still offer no runtime, so `serve run` fails each job as it starts and no job of its own has a result yet
- `serve run` runs each backtest job in a worker process of its own, a process of the same binary that runs the one job it is handed: it keeps one started for each job slot, hands a job to one over its standard input, and kills it once the job ends, so no job runs where another did; a worker gets an environment with nothing of the server's in it, no store, database URL, credentials or descriptor but its three pipes, and a scratch directory of its own, removed once it stops
- `--job-cpu-secs` (`QF_JOB_CPU_SECS`, 600), `--job-memory-mib` (`QF_JOB_MEMORY_MIB`, 4096, address space) and `--job-wall-secs` (`QF_JOB_WALL_SECS`, 1800) limit each job; a job past one is stopped and fails as `invalid_request`, its message naming the limit, and `serve run` prints `job_limits:`
- on Linux a worker can neither create a socket nor leave its process group (seccomp), so a process a job starts goes with it, and reaches no file outside its scratch directory (Landlock, where the kernel has it), and `serve run` and its workers are not dumpable, so no other process of their user can read their memory or environment; macOS enforces a job's CPU time and Windows only its wall-clock time; `serve run` prints `worker_isolation:` with what its workers enforce, and `docs/worker-isolation.md` says what each guard covers and what none does yet
- a worker that crashes or is killed fails its own job, `internal`, saying how it stopped, and the server runs the next job on a fresh worker; a cancelled job's worker is killed at once, and a worker exits as soon as its server goes away
- `server::WorkerPool` is the pool, a `JobWorker`, with `server::run_worker` and the hidden `quantforge worker` command as the worker's side; this release's workers offer no runtime, so each job still fails as it starts
- `serve run` queues backtests of Python strategy bundles in its store: `PUT /v1/bundles/{code_hash}` stores a bundle's canonical tar under its code hash, `POST /v1/backtests` checks and queues a job, `GET /v1/backtests/{job_id}` and `GET /v1/backtests` (the last submitted first, `limit` 1 to 1,000, default 100) say where jobs stand, and `POST /v1/backtests/{job_id}/cancel` cancels a queued or running one; `protocol::jobs` specifies the routes, with `BundleReceipt` and `JobList`, and `JobStatus` carries the submission as the server accepted it
- an upload is refused as `invalid_request` unless it is sent as `application/x-tar`, fits the bundle spec's default limits (1 MiB a file, 8 MiB in all, 256 files), hashes to the name it was sent under, and reads back exactly as the canonical tar of a bundle that passes the spec and, on a server with a worker runtime, that runtime's admission; a bundle's refusal lists its problems word for word
- a submission is refused as `invalid_request`, before anything is queued, unless its market and range are in their canonical forms, its range spans no more bars than `--max-span-bars`, its cash is above zero and its fee zero or more, it names a bundle the server holds and, on a server with a worker runtime, that runtime admits, and its range lies within the bars the server stores; a market without `category` reads Spot unless another family holds the symbol and interval, and the queued job names its category
- jobs start in the order they were submitted, `--job-concurrency` (`QF_JOB_CONCURRENCY`; half the CPU cores, at least 1, unless set) at a time, each on the candles the server read for it as it started; a worker never sees the store, its URL or its credentials, and `serve run` prints `job_concurrency:` before `listening:`. This release has no worker runtime, so it checks no bundle against one: a bundle that passes the spec is stored and jobs naming it are queued, and each job fails as it starts, `unavailable`, saying so
- a job's life is kept in the selected store, SQLite or PostgreSQL: its ID, its state (`queued`, `running`, `succeeded`, `failed` or `cancelled`), its timestamps, the submission with its code hash, its results and why it failed; a claim and every move is one guarded change, so a job is never claimed twice and never leaves a final state
- cancelling a running job stops its worker and frees its slot for the next job, a queued one never starts, and cancelling a finished job is `409 conflict`
- a job that finds the store out of time as it starts or ends asks again, a second apart, up to ten times; one that still cannot start fails `unavailable`, worth submitting again, and one whose end still cannot be written stays `running`, with the error logged, until the server next starts and fails it like any job left running
- `serve run` stopping on a signal fails the jobs it runs, `internal`, "the server stopped while the job ran; submit it again", and leaves the queued ones queued; a server that was killed fails them so as it starts again, then runs the queued ones. One server runs a store's queue
- `JobStore` is the storage port for bundles and jobs, implemented by both backends and held to the shared conformance group `jobs`, racing claimers included; `data migrate` copies the new `bundles` and `jobs` tables, and `--verify` compares them
- `server::JobWorker` is the seam a worker runtime plugs into, handed each job's input and a cancel signal; `server::NoRuntime` is a worker that offers no runtime
- `serve run` serves the history its store holds: `GET /v1/markets` lists every market with the open times of its first and last candle, `GET /v1/candles` answers one market's range as the segments that hold it and its newest candles inline, and `GET /v1/segments/{hash}` sends a segment's stored bytes; `protocol::segments` specifies the three routes, with `MarketIndex`, `CandleRangeQuery` and `CandleRange`
- reading a range's segments and tip as the protocol describes gives back exactly the candles the store holds, on SQLite and on PostgreSQL, every decimal with its scale and every bar where the store has one
- a segment is sent with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable` and the entity tag `"<hash>"`, a request whose `If-None-Match` names that tag is answered `304 Not Modified`, and a stored segment that no longer matches its hash is never sent
- a market the store holds nothing of, or a range holding no candles, is an empty answer whose `message` says which, never an error
- a `/v1/candles` query without `category`, the form markets took before they carried one, reads Spot, and is refused as ambiguous once the exchange holds the symbol and interval in another family too; every answer names its category
- `--max-span-bars` (`QF_MAX_SPAN_BARS`, default 527,040, a leap year of `1m` bars) bounds the bars one `/v1/candles` answer, or one backtest job, may span; a longer range is refused as `invalid_request`, and like `--listen` the flag reads its variable only when it is absent
- a `/v1/candles` range whose tip, the candles not packed into segments yet, holds more than 44,640 bars (31 windows, a month of `1m` bars) is refused as `invalid_request`, asking for a shorter range, and never answered with the tip cut short
- the history and job routes call the store on blocking threads, each call bounded by the storage route budget, behind as many turns as the store's pool has connections, which the job queue shares: a request that gets no turn in time, or whose store runs out of time, is answered `503 unavailable` with `Retry-After`, a store failure `500 internal`, and neither answer names the store's host, database or messages, which go to the log
- the server answers every refusal with the protocol's error envelope: `404 not_found` for a path it does not answer, `400 unsupported_version` with the versions it speaks for a path under another version, and `405 method_not_allowed`, a new `ErrorCode`, with `Allow`
- `CandleStore::market_coverage` lists the markets a store holds candles of and the open times each spans, in two indexed steps per market, and leaves out, with a warning, a market stored under a name the build cannot read back
- ci runs the whole test suite against PostgreSQL 16, 17 and 18, the official images as service containers, one serving TLS and one with `fsync` off; `QUANTFORGE_TEST_POSTGRES_REQUIRED=1` turns a test that would skip for want of a server into a failure, a leg that leaves a database or role behind fails, and the `storage` check passes only when every leg passed (`docs/ci.md`)
- ci checks the SQLite-only build (`--no-default-features`) with clippy and the tests on every platform, and checks the crate with Rust 1.85, its declared minimum, in the `package` check
- `scripts/postgres-test-servers.sh` runs ci's two PostgreSQL servers locally with Docker and prints the variables the tests read
- `quantforge data migrate --from <file>` copies a SQLite store into the selected PostgreSQL store: candles, runs, order events, closed trades, segments, the segment index and its dirty marks, every value as stored, decimal text and scale, IDs, sequence numbers, timestamps, JSON and legacy identities included; the source is read from one snapshot, read-only, and never changed, and the target keeps a store ID of its own, stays inactive, and is bound to the source's environment
- `data migrate --verify` compares every row of the copy with the source and prints, for each table and market, both sides' rows, key bounds and canonical SHA-256 checksum, then each difference; any difference exits with status 1
- a copy cut off is finished by running the same command again, which writes each row once, and a finished copy writes nothing; until a copy finishes, every command but `data migrate` and `store status` refuses its target, and `store status` prints `transfer:`
- while a copy is unfinished, its PostgreSQL target refuses every write but the copy's own, from any process, so one that had the store open before the copy began cannot slip rows into it; the copy begins only after finding every transferred table empty, with writers held off
- a resumed copy first compares the rows already copied with the source and refuses, before writing anything, one the source has since changed or deleted: that copy cannot be finished, and starts over in a new, empty database
- `data migrate` refuses, before writing anything, a source whose runs are starting or running (pass `--assume-quiescent` once nothing writes to it), a source at an earlier schema, and a target that holds rows of its own or another store's copy; a source that changes while it is read fails the copy, which stays unfinished
- `docs/storage-migration.md` covers switching to PostgreSQL and going back, why the SQLite file is no rollback once PostgreSQL has accepted writes, and the canonical text the checksums are taken over, which `tests/fixtures/storage_transfer_cases.json`, written by an independent Python oracle, holds both stores to
- `--storage postgres` keeps everything in PostgreSQL: `data`, `backtest`, `trade`, `monitor`, `store` and `serve run` all open the selected store, with candles, segments, runs and the store's record there, and no SQLite file is created, opened or read; SQLite stays the default, unchanged
- the first command on an empty PostgreSQL database creates the schema and warns that it did; `docs/configuration.md` says what the database and its role need
- `store status` and `serve run` print `storage: <backend> <location>`, and every command logs it; a database shows as `postgres://HOST:PORT/DATABASE`, never with its user or password
- a command that cannot open its store names it, and a PostgreSQL connection that fails says why: the server refused the URL's user or password, the database does not exist, or what stopped the connection
- `docs/storage-durability.md` is the contract both backends keep: an `Ok` from a write means committed and flushed, what that survives (an application crash, a database crash, an operating system crash, a power loss), what it assumes of the disk, how long a call may take, and which crashes have actually been tried
- `trade run --mode live` and `trade close` refuse, before any request, a store that cannot keep its commits, naming the setting and the remedy; other commands warn and run, and `quantforge store status` prints `durability: met` or `durability: weakened` with what it read back
- `StorageError::kind()` gives every storage failure one category on both backends; `StorageError::Contention` is a writer held up past its budget and `StorageError::Conflict` a deadlock or serialization failure, and neither changed anything. `storage::retry_conflicts` runs a transaction again within a caller's budget, and only after a conflict
- `AnyStore::within(budget)` bounds every call through it, waits and statements together, sharing the same file or pool; `StorageLimits::route_budget` is the 2 s webhook admission (ALG-743) will hand it, leaving a second of TradingView's three
- `PostgresStore` implements `SegmentStore` with SQLite's answers: a window is read in one snapshot, packed with no transaction open, and written back only if its mark still carries the revision read, so a correction after the snapshot is never published as clean; segments live in the database as `BYTEA`, and a replaced segment stays stored. `AnyStore` can hold a `PostgresStore`
- `docs/storage-schema.md` states the rules a later command must follow to reclaim replaced segments: only a segment no index entry and no retained provenance record names, in one transaction that keeps write-backs out, and only when an operator runs it; no release deletes a segment
- `PostgresStore` implements `RunJournalStore` and `StoreControl` with SQLite's rules and messages: runs created once and saved only after, the latest run by update, start and then run ID compared byte by byte whatever the database's collation, order events and closed trades appended raw and listed newest first, and the store's binding, legacy runs and their adoption
- `docs/storage-schema.md` states the journal's write contract: each write stands alone, a run's position is saved before its order event and never rolled back by a failed append, nothing is retried below the caller, one process drives a run at a time and the last save wins, and closed trades keep their Spot meaning
- `PostgresStore` implements `CandleStore` with SQLite's semantics: a batch is one transaction that writes its candles as one statement and marks the segment windows it changed with a fresh revision, so a refresh never publishes a window over candles it did not pack; identical candles change no row
- `PostgresStore::init` creates the PostgreSQL schema, schema 6 with SQLite's tables and exact `TEXT` decimals, in one transaction under an advisory lock: processes starting together create it once, a failure leaves nothing behind, and running it again changes nothing; a later build migrates it in place, one step per transaction
- a PostgreSQL database from a newer build, or one whose schema holds tables named like QuantForge's but records no schema version, is refused untouched
- `docs/postgres-query-plans.md` records, on representative data, the index each storage query is planned on in PostgreSQL, and why the PostgreSQL schema leaves out SQLite's copy of the candles key and builds no BRIN index
- `docs/storage-conformance.md` and `tests/storage/conformance/`: the shared storage conformance suite every backend passes, with candle, journal and segment scenarios read from `tests/fixtures/storage_conformance_cases.json`, whose answers an independent reference model, `spikes/storage-contract/conformance_cases.py`, writes, and the dirty-window races each backend steps through itself; SQLite passes it directly and through `AnyStore`, and `tests/storage/postgres.rs` gives every fixture a PostgreSQL database of its own, ready for the PostgreSQL stores
- `--storage` (`QF_STORAGE`) selects the storage backend, `sqlite` (the default, at `--db`) or `postgres`, whose connection comes from `--postgres-url` (`QF_POSTGRES_URL`), `--postgres-pool-size` (`QF_POSTGRES_POOL_SIZE`; 2, or 8 for `serve run`) and `--postgres-connect-timeout-secs` (`QF_POSTGRES_CONNECT_TIMEOUT_SECS`, 5); a selection never falls back to SQLite
- a PostgreSQL setting with no backend selected, and `--db` or `QF_DB` with `--storage postgres`, are refused before anything is created, naming the flag or variable that set them
- the PostgreSQL URL takes libpq's form and its `sslmode` and `sslrootcert`; plain text, or a fallback to it, is refused for any host but this machine, every other parameter is refused, and its password never appears in `--help`, a log line, an error or a `Debug` output
- `StorageSettings::resolve` turns what a host's flags and variables held into checked settings for one backend, `StoreFactory` opens the store they describe through the provider registered for its backend, and `AnyStore` serves every storage port for whichever backend it holds
- `storage::postgres`, built with the default-on `postgres` cargo feature, is the bridge of ADR 0001: tokio-postgres and a bounded deadpool pool on a runtime the store owns, a deadline on every call with the query cancelled past it, TLS through rustls; `PostgresStore` connects and answers, and implements no storage port yet; `--no-default-features` builds SQLite only
- `StorageError::Unavailable` (nothing changed; safe to retry) and `StorageError::OutcomeUnknown` (a write that lost its answer; check before retrying)
- a SQLite database from any earlier release is migrated in place when it is opened: schema 1 (0.1.0), 2 (0.2.0) and the development schemas 3, 4 and 5 move forward to schema 6 step by step, each step in one transaction, and `docs/upgrading.md` is the operator's guide; nothing is deleted, and the original JSON of every migrated run and order event stays beside its migrated form
- `quantforge store status`, `store bind` and `store adopt-run`: the database's own record — its identity, the environment it is bound to for life (`mainnet`, `testnet` or `demo`, declared by the operator and checked against the base URL, never inferred from it), the migrations it went through, and the runs an earlier release wrote without a verified identity, which an operator adopts before they can be resumed or closed
- every run records the identity it started under (the store's environment and, for a live run, the fingerprint of the API key), and `trade run --run-id` and `trade close` drive a run only under the identity that recorded it; `monitor status` prints `run_identity`
- `docs/storage-schema.md`: the storage contract — identities, the store activation contract that ALG-738, ALG-767 and ALG-768 implement, enforce and verify, schema ownership, the SQLite DDL and the reviewed PostgreSQL DDL — and `tests/storage/` with the shared identity cases of `tests/fixtures/storage_identity_cases.json`
- `ExchangeId` names product lines: beside `binance_spot` come `binance_linear`, `binance_inverse`, `bybit_spot`, `bybit_linear` and `bybit_inverse`, each a `Venue` (`binance`, `bybit`) with a `Category` (`spot`, `linear`, `inverse`), and `MarketId::category()` gives a market's family; only Binance Spot has an adapter so far, and the CLI is unchanged
- `model` gains execution identity and units: `Environment`, `InstrumentId`, `AccountRef`, venue order, algo-order and trade IDs as text, and `OperationId`; `Price`, `BaseQty`, whole `Contracts`, `Quantity`, `FaceValue`, `Leverage` and `PositionSide`; `Money`, whose arithmetic refuses to mix assets; and `Refusal`, the parity matrix's refusal vocabulary
- `model::futures` types the futures contract of ADR 0002: instruments with their trading rules and universe rule 1, accounts, settings and positions, entry and reduce-only orders, acknowledgements that carry no status or fills, fills, stops and the read-back that alone makes a position protected, ledger records, private events, and QuantForge's 36-character client order IDs
- `ExternalIntent` and `PositionCycle` are the records a store will keep, and their `IntentState` and `ProtectionState` move only on the venue's evidence: an acknowledgement never fills an intent, a "not found" settles an entry only past the 30-second propagation delay with the position still flat, and nothing is protected or closed without a read-back or snapshot that shows it
- `RealizedPnl` keeps gross, fees, funding and net PnL apart in the settlement asset, each with its source and provisional until final; a figure the venue has not reported yet stays pending, never zero, and Spot's `ClosedTrade.gross_quote_pnl` is not reused
- `ports::futures` splits the futures connector ports by what each may do: only `EntryConnector` opens exposure, `ProtectionConnector` can only reduce it, and `FuturesCapabilities` describes how a venue carries the contract without ever marking an operation unsupported; `ports::Clock` is the injected time source
- `cargo test --test parity` checks the intent, position and accounting state machines, the refusal vocabulary and the frozen policy values against the code; `tests/exchange_boundary.rs` keeps each venue's own position fields inside its adapter; `docs/shared-model.md` lists what changes for Spot, the Python bindings and the wire, and what does not

- `docs/parity/` freezes the v0.3 capability parity matrix: `matrix.toml` lists every function QuantForge 0.3 promises on Binance and Bybit, with SQLite and PostgreSQL, as 458 required cells over venue, store, product family, side, surface and artifact, each with an objective pass condition, implementation and evidence stories, the evidence it needs and the contract its area follows; `cells.txt` ledgers the cells so that coverage only shrinks through a recorded scope change, `evidence.jsonl` takes pass or fail records only, the dated universe list records what rule 1 selected, and `cargo test --test parity` checks all of it against the code's own states and error kinds and renders `matrix.md`

- ADR 0002 decides the v0.3 automation architecture: TradingView alerts reach a Rust executor beside, not inside, the candle strategies, which stay unchanged; it fixes instrument, account, environment and position identity, the product universe (USDT-settled linear and coin-settled inverse perpetuals on Binance and Bybit), one-way mode with explicit arming, risk sizing in each instrument's settlement asset, mark-price stops held by the exchange, the response to a failed stop, stale-alert and ownership rules, and what parity across venues and stores means; `docs/automation-requirements.md` maps the automation PRD's requirements to the stories that deliver them, `docs/futures-venues.md` records each venue's products, routes, recent API changes and open questions, and `spikes/futures-contract/` resolves the product universe from public metadata and checks the sizing examples independently

- `docs/adr/` records architecture decisions; ADR 0001 decides how the synchronous storage ports will reach PostgreSQL: tokio-postgres with a deadpool pool on a small runtime the store owns, so no context that calls the ports can hit a nested-runtime panic, rustls with `ring` for TLS, a default-on `postgres` cargo feature, and PostgreSQL 16 to 18; compiling spikes in `spikes/postgres-bridge/` back each rule, and SQLite-only builds are unaffected

- candle segments: a market's closed candles are packed window by window, 1,440 bars of the interval counted from the Unix epoch (one UTC day of `1m` candles), into immutable segments named `sha256:` and the hash of their payload; `docs/segment-spec.md` specifies segment format 1 and records why it is not Arrow IPC
- `storage::segments` implements the format: `encode` writes a window's candles as columns, with every decimal as its exact text so that `1.10` and `1.1` pack differently, `decode` refuses any payload `encode` would not write, `compress` and `decompress` wrap the payload in exactly one zstd frame, and `open` reads a fetched segment back, checking it against its name
- `SegmentStore` is the port for packed segments, and `SqliteStore` implements it: `refresh_segments` packs every closed window whose candles changed since it was last packed, `resolve_segments` resolves a range of open times to the segments that hold it and the tip after them, whose candles are read from the candle store, and `load_segment` returns a segment's stored bytes by name
- writing candles marks the windows whose candles changed, in the same transaction, so a refresh repacks exactly those, and a plan never serves a segment older than its candles; a window that cannot be packed, such as one holding more than 1,440 candles, stays unpacked, keeps every later window in the tip, and is logged as a warning naming `data validate`
- `data sync` packs the market's segments after its first pass and after every pass that writes candles, and prints `segments_built: N`; `DataSyncEngine::with_segments` turns this on for library callers
- `tests/fixtures/segment_cases.json` holds the segment spec's shared cases for every implementation, with payloads and names computed by an independent Python implementation

- `serve run` starts the server other QuantForge instances connect to: it listens on `--listen` (`QF_LISTEN`, default `127.0.0.1:9600`, an IP address and a port; port 0 picks a free one) and prints the address it listens on as `listening: http://…`
- the server answers `GET /healthz` with `ok` while it runs, and `GET /version` with a `protocol::ServerVersion`: its release and the protocol versions it speaks, in a shape that is the same in every protocol version, so that a client can choose its version before its first versioned request
- `serve run` stops on SIGINT or SIGTERM (Ctrl-C on Windows): it stops accepting connections, finishes the requests in progress, prints `shutdown:` and `stopped:` lines, and exits 0; a second signal stops it without waiting for them, and it exits 1

- `protocol` defines the wire format between QuantForge instances as serde types: a backtest job's submission (a Python bundle named by its content hash, the candles it runs on, and the backtest settings), its status and result references, the error envelope, candle segment index entries, and the live stream's frames; its rustdoc is the wire specification, and every JSON example in it is a doctest
- the protocol is versioned: `protocol::PROTO_VERSION` and `protocol::MIN_SUPPORTED` bound the versions a build speaks, an HTTP request names its version in its path (`/v1/…`), and `VersionRange::negotiate` picks the newest version two peers share or says which side is too old; `stream::HelloVersions` reads the versions out of a hello of any version, so that a server can refuse a client whose version it does not speak with its own range
- protocol decimals travel as JSON strings and are read exactly: a JSON number, an exponent, or a value a decimal cannot hold without rounding is refused
- a frame type, error code, gap reason, or job state a receiver does not know reads as `Unrecognized` instead of failing, so that a version can grow without breaking older peers
- `tests/protocol_boundary.rs` compiles `src/protocol/` on its own and scans its sources, so that the module uses nothing else from the crate and depends only on `serde`, `serde_json`, `rust_decimal`, `sha2`, `thiserror`, and `toml`
- `docs/bundle-spec.md` specifies the strategy bundle a remote backtest runs: explicit files and a `quantforge.toml` manifest naming the entry module and class, the engine versions and Python version it runs on, its parameters, and its pinned packages — never a pickled object
- `protocol::bundle` implements the spec: `Bundle::from_files` and `Bundle::from_dir` check a bundle against the path rules, the allowed extensions (`.py`, `.toml`, `.json`, `.txt`), the size limits of `BundleLimits`, and the manifest, and report every problem with a shared code and a message that says what to change
- a bundle's code hash is the SHA-256 of its canonical tar, which sorts the files and fixes every timestamp, owner, and mode, so the same files hash the same on every machine; `Bundle::code_hash` gives it, and `Bundle::canonical_tar` the bytes a client uploads
- `Bundle::from_canonical_tar` reads an upload back and refuses any archive that is not exactly the canonical tar of its files, and `Bundle::admit` refuses a bundle whose engine, Python version, or pinned packages the server's `Runtime` does not offer
- `tests/fixtures/bundle_cases.json` holds the spec's shared cases for every implementation, the Python SDK's included, with code hashes computed by an independent Python implementation

- `exchange::conformance` writes down what every exchange connector must do for the engines as checks: candles on the UTC-millisecond grid with exact decimals and closed-bar semantics, kline window bounds, paging, and gaps, the symbol rules and the trading rules they impose, the market-order lifecycle, the error kinds, and the exchange clock; `exchange::conformance::run` runs a check against any connector factory and refuses a production endpoint before sending anything
- `tests/conformance/` runs every check against every registered exchange, replayed by `cargo test` from fixtures recorded on the testnet or, for the fault checks no testnet produces on demand, written by hand; `CONFORMANCE_MODE=testnet` runs every other check live against the testnet, and `CONFORMANCE_MODE=record` re-records the fixtures through a proxy that strips the credentials, signatures, and account id, and writes only checks that replay exactly as they ran
- each check is proven able to fail: a control corrupts the connector the way that check exists to catch, and the check must fail naming it
- `ExchangeErrorKind` classifies every `ExchangeError` in terms every exchange shares (`ExchangeError::kind`), and tells a request the exchange refused before processing it (`unavailable`, safe to resend) from one that may have taken effect (`outcome_unknown`, so an order is looked up before it is sent again), and a server error is always `outcome_unknown`, whatever its code; Binance maps its documented error codes and HTTP statuses onto the kinds
- `ExchangeError::api` builds the error an exchange reported
- `docs/exchange-feature-matrix.md`, generated from the fixtures, lists each exchange's capabilities beside its conformance results, and `docs/adding-an-exchange.md` describes adding an exchange
- a scheduled `conformance` workflow runs the suite's live checks against Binance Spot testnet every day, outside the pull-request checks

- `--exchange` selects the exchange by its registry name, with `QF_EXCHANGE` as its fallback and `binance` as the default; any other value fails before anything runs, listing the registered exchanges
- the registry names its exchanges: `exchange::lookup` and `exchange::name` map between an `ExchangeId` and the name operators select it by, `exchange::registered` lists every exchange, `exchange::DEFAULT_EXCHANGE` is the default, and `exchange::default_base_url` gives each one's production endpoint
- `tests/exchange_boundary.rs` also fails when code or `--help` outside `src/exchange/binance/` names Binance beyond the registry entry, the `ExchangeId` variant, and the refused old base-URL names

- `ExchangeCapabilities` describes what an exchange supports — market orders sized by base or quote quantity, client order ids, the candle page size, symbol rules, server time, and streams — so callers branch on capabilities instead of on `ExchangeId`; Binance Spot declares its own
- `exchange::connect` builds an exchange's connectors from an `ExchangeId` and a `ConnectorConfig` through a registry that maps every exchange id to a factory; `ExchangeConnectors::signed` returns the trading and account connectors only when credentials were supplied
- `MarketDataConnector::server_time` reads the exchange clock, `GET /api/v3/time` on Binance
- `tests/exchange_boundary.rs` fails when any code outside `src/exchange/` names a concrete exchange adapter

- `BacktestEngine` documents its execution model — bar-close signals, next-open fills, the basis-point fee on every fill, close-out at the last close, close-marked equity — and its determinism guarantee, with a runnable example that backtests a strategy defined outside the crate

- `CandleBatch` holds candles as parallel columns — `open_time_ms`, `close_time_ms`, `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `volume`, and `trades` — for the planned Python bindings, and converts to and from `Vec<Candle>` without loss; `CandleBatch::from_columns` builds one from a `CandleColumns` and rejects columns of different lengths
- `decimal_to_f64` and `decimal_from_f64` implement the decimal ↔ float64 policy in `docs/decimal-float-policy.md`: the nearest float64 one way, the shortest round-trip decimal the other, and an error instead of rounding for NaN, infinities, and values a decimal cannot hold; `tests/fixtures/decimal_float_cases.csv` pins the cases for Rust and Python alike

### Fixed

- reading a stored decimal that a `Decimal` cannot hold exactly, with more than 28 decimal places, was silently rounded on SQLite; candles and closed trades now refuse it with the text named, as every backend does
- refreshing a market's segments failed on a stored candle whose trade count was above 9223372036854775807, so no later window of that market was packed again; such a count is now packed as stored, as the segment format allows

- `data sync` stored the candle still forming at the end of each pass and never fetched it again, so a following sync kept a snapshot of nearly every candle taken seconds after it opened; it now stores a candle only once it has settled, and fetches the forming one again on a later pass

- `trade close --run-id` refuses a run recorded for a different exchange or symbol instead of selling this command's symbol against that run's position, and settles every refusal before contacting the exchange

- a backtest whose fill or equity arithmetic leaves the decimal range (for example an entry at a price so small that the quantity cannot be represented) returns the new `EngineError::Overflow` instead of panicking

- `Debug` output of `ApiCredentials`, and of any `BinanceSpotClient`, `ConnectorConfig`, or `ExchangeConnectors` holding them, no longer prints the API key or secret
- `Interval::H8` now formats as `8h` instead of `1m`
- re-sync any 8h candles written before this fix; they were stored under the `1m` interval key
- `Symbol` deserialization now validates and normalizes (trim, uppercase, reject empty) exactly like `Symbol::new`, closing the bypass on `state_json`/`raw_json` reloads
- zero-valued exchange filter limits (Binance renders "no constraint" as `0.00000000`, as its testnet does for `MARKET_LOT_SIZE`) are treated as absent rules, so quantity rounding falls back to `LOT_SIZE` instead of receiving a zero step
